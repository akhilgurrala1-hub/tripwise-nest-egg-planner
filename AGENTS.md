## Hosting
- The app is hosted on **Vercel** and its database and accounts are a **Supabase** project the owner controls (`wdphpwvkkfflwgwxazld`, us-east-1). It no longer runs on Lovable.
- Build: plain `vite.config.ts` (TanStack Start + `nitro({ preset: "vercel" })`), output in `.vercel/output`. Server code runs as a Node.js Vercel Function (Fluid Compute).
- Public Supabase values live in the committed `.env`; secrets (`GEMINI_API_KEY`, `SERPAPI_API_KEY`, `EIA_API_KEY`, optional `AI_*` overrides) are Vercel environment variables, and `.env.local` locally (git-ignored). Set `VITE_SITE_URL` to the production domain for social previews.

## Project rules
- Trip state lives in a client store (`src/lib/trip-store.ts`) persisted to localStorage; signed-in users can also save trips to their account (`src/lib/account.ts`), but every page must keep working signed out.
- All budget math is pure functions in `src/lib/budget.ts` with tests in `src/test/budget.test.ts`; UI must never compute totals itself so rules stay testable.
- The emergency reserve is carved out of the total budget before spendable money is computed and is never reduced by over-spending.
- Road-trip stops are excluded from taxes and the misc cushion so a stop's budget impact equals its cost exactly; over-budget cut suggestions are verified by recomputing the budget, not by estimates.
- Route data uses the `RouteStop` shape in `src/lib/routing/`. The Road trip page shows sample stops (demo provider, labeled as examples) until the traveler taps "Find real stops", which runs `findOpenRoute()` (`src/lib/routing/open-route.ts`, pure parsers tested in `src/test/open-route.test.ts`) from the browser for everyone, signed out included: places found by Open-Meteo geocoding (the trip's own points win), the road from public OSRM servers (project-osrm, then FOSSGIS), and stops within 4 km of it (1.2 km for meals) from OpenStreetMap through Overpass, spread along the route, with detour minutes estimated from distance off the road. Prices are honest: `fee=no` and a "$" `charge` are listed, free kinds (viewpoints, parks) are estimates, attractions with no fee stay "not listed" and are never counted as free. The map is MapLibre over OpenFreeMap's free tiles (`OpenRouteMap.tsx`, loaded only on that page). There is no Google Maps anywhere: it was billed through Lovable connector credits. The itinerary's real places are the free travel-guide, Wikipedia and OpenStreetMap plan for everyone; unpublished prices stay marked "not listed" and are never counted as free.
- Route and stop lookups run only on explicit user action (the Find real stops button), are cached for the session (React Query, and the last search is kept while moving between pages), and are bounded (one geocode per place, one route, one Overpass query) to be polite to the free services.
- Accounts use email/password via Supabase Auth with a single app-wide session subscriber (`AuthProvider` in `src/lib/auth-context.tsx`); no profiles table.
- Saved trips are migrated by version in `src/lib/trip/migrate.ts`; bump the version and add a migration when the stored shape changes.
- Example prices and venues live only in `src/lib/demo-data.ts`. The sample plan's venues are made up, so it is labeled "Example plan" (with the free Use real places one tap away) and example prices are labeled "Estimated"; never use the word "demo" in the UI. Labels are quiet `SourceNote` text, not badges.
- AI itinerary generation runs server-side via an auth-protected `createServerFn` in `src/lib/ai-itinerary.functions.ts` (it uses the app's shared free Gemini quota, so it is never callable anonymously) calling the AI helpers in `src/lib/ai/`; AI output is labeled as unverified estimates.
- No real bookings or payments are implemented.
- Theming: all colors are semantic tokens in `src/styles.css` with `:root` (light) and `.dark` values: a light paper tinted from the brand blue (`oklch(0.968 0.008 255)`, white cards) and deep navy in dark mode (`oklch(0.165 0.024 258)`), never cream or pure black, near-black `ink`, blue `primary` for UI accents, green `go` (`variant="go"` buttons) for actions that move the trip forward (Plan trip, Start planning, Plan your own trip). One blue accent does most of the work; trip types keep their hue (`--trip-*`) and travel styles a soft tint of theirs; tints stay light (`color-mix` at 5–16%), never white-on-color badges. Interest icons are `INTEREST_ICON` in `bits.tsx`. No gradients or glows, no pill-shaped chips or badges (buttons are capsules, like apple.com; chips and tags are rounded rectangles) and no cartoon art: decoration is line art in the brand's motif (`Illustrations.tsx`) plus the destination's real photo. Text on photos uses `on-image`; text on `bg-ink` uses `ink-foreground`. The theme class is set by an inline head script before paint to avoid flashes.
- Brand: the mark is a route drawn as a "W" from a hollow origin ring to a coral destination dot on a blue tile (`src/components/app/Logo.tsx`; brand blue `#2457E6` = `--primary`, coral `#FF6B4A` = `--brand-coral`, fixed across themes). Use coral only for "next / destination" cues. `public/favicon.svg` is the source of truth for every icon: after changing the mark, update `Logo.tsx` to match and run `node scripts/brand-assets.mjs` (Playwright; set `GEIST_DIR` to a folder of Geist woff2 files for the social image) to regenerate favicon.ico, touch/PWA icons and og-image.png. Branded loading uses `<LogoMark loading />`.
- Motion is spring physics (`src/lib/spring.ts`, tested: exact damped-oscillator solutions, `projectMomentum`, `rubberBand`, `snapIndex`; `SPRINGS` snappy/smooth/bouncy/press) driven by `src/components/app/motion.tsx`: `createBoxSpring` / `useActiveIndicator` (interruptible, velocity handoff), `Segmented` (the highlight responds on pointer-down, drags 1:1, rubber-bands past the ends, snaps to the projected option; arrow keys), `useTweened`/`AnimatedMoney` (spring numbers), `useDragScroll` (rails: drag, momentum, edge bounce). CSS uses the same springs as `linear()` easings (`--ease-spring-*`, `spring-snappy/smooth/bouncy`; the design-system test keeps them equal to `SPRINGS`), `pressable` for instant press feedback. Content is simply there: no fade-ins, parallax or scroll effects on the hero, cards, sections or page changes, and no count-ups (tested); numbers only spring when the traveler changes them. UI transitions stay at 300ms or less (springs 250 to 300ms, press release 200ms), the switch doesn't bounce, nothing grows from `scale(0)`, idle loops are gone from the travel scenes and the logo has no hover ripple (Emil Kowalski's rules). Linked idea-card photos zoom 1.03 inside their frame on hover (pointer devices only). Reduced motion keeps quick crossfades (opacity/color only) and stops everything that moves. The opening animation (`Intro.tsx`) is pure CSS, plays once per session and is skipped for reduced motion.
- Itinerary edits are pure functions in `src/lib/itinerary-edit.ts` (add, update, move, move to day, remove/restore for Undo) with tests in `src/test/itinerary-edit.test.ts`; the store wraps them. Items the traveler adds or edits get `custom: true` ("Yours") and `keepCustomItems()` carries them over whenever a plan is rebuilt (demo, AI or real places), so suggestions never wipe the traveler's own stops.
- CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs type check, lint, tests and build on every PR and push to main; keep it green.
- Never compute dates at module scope: edge runtimes freeze the clock at 1970 during module load. Sample trips are built lazily in `trip-store.ts` (`getSample`).
- Live data lives in `src/lib/live/`. Weather (Open-Meteo forecast within 16 days, last year's same dates beyond that, labeled "typical") and place autocomplete (Open-Meteo geocoding) are free and keyless, so they run client-side for everyone; never call them during SSR. Each demo destination has a real `anchor` town for weather; custom destinations never inherit it.
- Searches (places, hotels, AI) use `destinationQuery()` from `src/lib/live/place.ts` so generic labels like "Custom destination" or "Staycation" never reach a search; a staycation searches the traveler's home city.
- A budget category is labeled "Live price" only while its input still equals the fetched price (`src/lib/live/sources.ts`); `PriceSourceBadge` shows what is live. The live-prices panel asks `getLivePriceStatus` which keys exist and never offers a search that can't run.
- Trip types are separate categories (`TRIP_TYPES` in `demo-data.ts`): road trip, fly-away, cruise, train & bus, staycation. Each lists its allowed transport modes (`modeForTripType` enforces them), destinations declare which types they suit in `bestFor`, and `categoriesFor()` in `budget.ts` decides which budget categories a type shows (cruise costs only on cruises, stops only on road trips, gas/tolls only when driving). The Road trip tab and page appear only for road trips.
- Cruises price cabin + main dining as a per-person-per-night fare plus gratuities and port fees in the `cruise` category (not taxed: port fees include taxes); hotel nights default to $0. Switching trip type goes through `tripActions.setTripType`, which re-estimates the budget and returns plain-language notes on what changed.
- Stored trips are version 7 (v6 added `bookings`, v7 `spending`); `migrate()` maps the old "budget-vacation" (Getaway) type to fly-away, train & bus or road trip by transport mode, and v5 gave every trip a stable `id` (kept through edits and trip-type switches; new for each trip started or opened; never saved to the account). Optional setup fields `originPoint` / `destinationPoint` need no migration.
- Open tabs stay in sync: the store listens for `storage` events on its key and adopts the other tab's trip, so tabs never overwrite each other with stale copies.
- Generated plans (AI, real places) are requested for a `planKey(trip)` (id, type, destination, dates) and applied with `tripActions.setItineraryFor(key, plan)`, which discards the answer if the trip changed mid-request. `usePlanner()` (`src/components/app/itinerary/use-planner.ts`) runs one job at a time with cancel, a 90s timeout and a "Try again" action; failures never replace the current plan. The AI server function also aborts when the client disconnects or after 85s.
- `useTrip()` derives budget, savings and cut plan once per trip change (cached by state object), so any number of components can read it cheaply.
- Page structure: nav follows the flow: Plan, Overview (`/dashboard`), Budget, Itinerary, Road trip (road trips only), Book. `PageHeader` shows the trip bar on every planning page (trip type switchable in place via a popover, destination, dates, travelers, Edit trip); account pages pass `showTrip={false}`. Choosing a trip type always goes through `TripTypePicker` so it looks and moves the same everywhere.
- Travel scenes (`TravelScene.tsx`, geometry in `travel-scene-geometry.ts`): car, plane, ship, train/bus or home, drawing the route from the origin ring to the coral destination dot. They play only when the trip type or mode changes (Web Animations from pure, tested keyframes), rest on the final frame that the server renders, and never move with reduced motion.
- `NumberField` accepts any amount (`step="any"`; arrow keys nudge to the next round step) so browser validation never blocks a form over an odd dollar amount.
- Plan vs. budget on the itinerary uses `compareToBudget()` (within 5% or $10 counts as matching) and offers a one-tap, undoable sync of the Activities budget.
- Each trip type has its own hue (`--trip-road`, `--trip-flight`, `--trip-cruise`, `--trip-rail`, `--trip-home`; `TRIP_COLOR` in `trip-colors.ts`), used for travel scenes, the trip chip, the Plan preview and booking icons. Brand blue stays the UI accent; coral stays the "next / destination" cue.
- Where the traveler starts (`src/lib/live/use-home.ts`): profile home city, else the device location (only after they tap "Use my location"; named with BigDataCloud's free client-side lookup), else the approximate city the host attaches to the request (`src/server.ts` reads Vercel's `x-vercel-ip-*` headers, or Cloudflare's `request.cf`, via `src/lib/live/visitor-geo.ts` and passes it on as the `x-waypoint-visitor-geo` header; `getVisitorLocation` reads it — no third-party IP lookup). The untouched sample trip is personalized to start there (`tripActions.personalizeSample`). On the Plan page, changing the start (typed, picked or located) moves the destination to the best fit from there via `destinationForNewStart()` until the traveler picks one; a typed city shows what it matched. "Use my location" retries once with high accuracy (never after a refusal) and falls back to the approximate city.
- Suggestions: real places with coordinates live in `src/data/places.json` and `src/data/cruises.json`, read by `src/lib/catalog.ts` (facts only; their prices are demo estimates built in `demo-data.ts`). Ids are `place:<id>` and `cruise:<id>`. `recommendDestinations()` (`src/lib/recommend.ts`) ranks by distance fit for the trip type, interests and the start point. Always resolve a trip's destination with `destinationFor(setup)`, which sets drive miles and example fares from the start point; `getDestination(id)` alone ignores distance.
- AI trip assistant (Plan page, `TripAssistant`): every message is first read by Waypoint's own understanding engine (`src/lib/understand/`, pure and free, runs in the browser), so the assistant works for everyone, signed out included, and never depends on the AI. `understand(turns)` folds the whole conversation into one draft (later answers win; bare answers like "2" or "chicago" go to the question they answer, using the `fields` each assistant turn asked about), `planQuestions()` asks what's missing first (where, when, how long, who) with tap answers that the reader understands, then up to two optional questions to go deeper (style, interests, cabin, transport, budget; each asked once), narrows a country or region to a city ("Where in Japan?"), asks "which one" for "X or Y", and offers ideas from `recommendDestinations()` for vague messages ("idk somewhere warm lol"). Signed in, the auth-protected `tripChat` server function is also asked, with Waypoint's reading sent as `facts` (`factLines`), and its answer always goes through `reconcile()`: cities the traveler named are put back, exact dates and group sizes they gave win, a budget they never gave is dropped, readiness is recomputed, and questions about things already said are replaced. If the AI fails for any reason, Waypoint answers on its own and says so. `setupFromDraft()` in `src/lib/assistant.ts` turns a ready draft into a setup. The conversation is kept in sessionStorage so it survives the sign-in round trip.
- The understanding engine's readers (`text.ts` typos and number words, `where.ts` places, multi-city routes, nights per city and origins, `when.ts` dates, holidays, seasons and lengths, `money.ts`, `people.ts`, `prefs.ts`) each take out what they used so the next can't misread it ("5 nights" is never a budget, "september 9 people" isn't a date). Places come from `gazetteer.ts`: countries, states, nicknames (`src/data/place-aliases.json`), the catalog and, once loaded, every airport town in English (plus short forms: "Frankfurt" for Frankfurt am Main, "Jeju" for Jeju City) and the towns table (`src/data/towns.json`, `useTowns`, below airport towns in weight, a bigger town winning a shared name); a town whose name is an everyday word ("Reading", "Long Mỹ") only counts with a state or country that contains it or in an "and"/"then" list, never from a capital letter alone; everyday words that are also towns ("nice", "split", "reunion", in `rules.json`) only count when capitalized, qualified or listed with other places, and short state codes that are words ("in", "me", "or") only qualify a city that really has that state.
- Assistant quality is measured, not guessed: `src/test/assistant-corpus.test.ts` scores the engine on 10,000 generated messages and conversations (`tools/corpus/assistant-prompts.jsonl`, written with independently worked-out answers by `python -m waypoint_data prompts`: vague, misspelled, lowercase, multi-city, families, budgets, holidays, origins, covered costs, multi-turn) and fails if any field drops below its bar (99.5% for most; currently 100%); `CORPUS_REPORT=1` prints misses. `src/test/assistant-golden.test.ts` has 50 hand-written messages the generator never produced, and `src/test/assistant-engine.test.ts` covers the conversation flow and the AI checks. When the assistant misreads something, add the message to the golden set, fix the reader, and keep both green.
- Booking (`/book`): `bookingPlan()` in `src/lib/booking.ts` lists what each trip type needs booked with provider searches pre-filled (dates, group size, place; KAYAK and Skyscanner on the exact route when both airport codes are known, Expedia for stays); travelers book on the provider's site and record status, confirmation and price in `trip.bookings`. Waypoint never books or takes payment. `src/lib/booking-timeline.ts` (pure, tested) gives each item a "book by" date and reason (`bookBy`: international flights about 2 months out, cruises 4, timed tickets 2 weeks...; labeled rules of thumb) and groups the page into Book now / Book this month / Later / Done (`bookingTimeline`); `prepChecklist()` adds "Before you go" items for trips abroad (passport validity through 6 months after return, entry rules, phone, cards, a driving permit with a rental car) and insurance for trips abroad, cruises or $3,000+, checked off in `trip.bookings` under `prep-*` keys; "Add to calendar" downloads an iCalendar file (`toICalendar`, RFC 5545: escaped text, folded lines) with the trip and a reminder for each open booking.
- Sign-up: the Supabase project should auto-confirm emails (Authentication → Sign In / Providers → Email → Confirm email off), so sign-up signs people in immediately and no confirmation email is sent.
- Budget rule: until the project is funded, add no paid APIs, keys or services. Prefer free, keyless open data called from the browser (never during SSR), cached with React Query.
- Worldwide: catalog places carry an ISO `country`; suggestions work from any start point. `src/lib/locale.ts` picks units (°F/miles only for the US and a few others) and a display currency from the traveler's country (home place, else the browser locale), overridable in the header's display menu (`setLocalePrefs`). All budget math stays in US dollars; other currencies only appear as an approximate conversion (`ApproxMoney`, Frankfurter / ECB rates). Rail and bus booking links follow the destination country (`railProviders` / `busProviders`).
- Free open data (`src/lib/live/open-data.ts`, hooks in `use-open-data.ts`): Wikipedia photo + introduction (credited, `DestinationPhoto` / `DestinationCard`), Wikivoyage travel-guide picks plus Wikipedia landmarks + OpenStreetMap restaurants for the free "Use real places" plan (itinerary `source: "open"`; their prices are never published, so they stay "not listed"), and Nager.Date public holidays during the trip (Overview and itinerary days).
- Print / Save as PDF: the itinerary has a print button (`window.print()`); hide controls in print with Tailwind's `print:hidden`, keep day cards `print:break-inside-avoid`.
- AI quality: the model isn't trained by the app. Answers are grounded in free real data (nearby Wikipedia landmarks and OpenStreetMap restaurants, real weather, public holidays; the trip assistant also gets the traveler's country, currency and nearby suggestions) and every answer goes through the tested clean-ups in `src/lib/ai-quality.ts` (`cleanAiPlan`: exact day count, known times/kinds, sorted, deduped, bounded prices and text, items naming a real place linked to the map; `cleanChatAnswer`) before it's shown. "Refine this plan" sends the traveler's requested changes plus the current plan (`planLines`). Grounding lookups are best-effort with a 5s cap and never block the AI call.
- `getLivePriceStatus` is public on purpose: it only reports which price sources have keys (no lookups, no cost), so nobody is asked to sign in for prices that can't be fetched. `getLivePrices` stays auth-protected. The Overview checklist only shows "Check live prices" when a source is connected.
- Menus use `<DropdownMenu modal={false}>` so the page stays usable (and accessible) while one is open. Run the axe-core audit (light, dark, mobile, plus open menus and dialogs) after UI changes; it should stay clean.
- Dependencies: zod is on v4; run `bun audit` before releases and apply in-range fixes (`bun audit fix`). The 24h `minimumReleaseAge` guard in `bunfig.toml` stays on.
- Writing: no em dashes anywhere in the UI (use a period, comma or colon), and no machine-sounding filler ("is live", "never counted as spending money"). AI answers are told not to use them and `plainDashes()` in `ai-quality.ts` strips any that slip through; Wikipedia intros go through `tidyExtract()` (drops pronunciations and asides before "is", e.g. "Nashville ( NASH-vil) is"). Wikipedia is credited by linking the photo and a "Read more" link, not a caption.
- Phones: bars over content (the top bar, the tab bar, the Plan page's sticky Plan trip bar, which sits above the tab bar) are the regular or thick material (frosted glass, opaque where `backdrop-filter` isn't supported). Form fields are 16px on small screens (global rule in `styles.css`) so iPhone Safari doesn't zoom in and leave the page cut off.
- The Overview shows the sample trip as a compact card with Plan yours (`PageHeader sampleBanner="card"`); other pages keep a one-line notice on phones and tablets (the sidebar says it on larger screens).
- Travel guide (`src/lib/live/travel-guide.ts`, `useTravelGuide` / `travelGuideQuery` in `use-open-data.ts`): Wikivoyage (free, keyless, CC BY-SA, called from the browser). `parseListings` reads See/Do/Buy/Eat/Drink listings with description, hours, address, coordinates and price; `priceToUsd` only counts a price that is clearly US dollars ("$" only where the local currency is USD), and Eat listings without a price get a typical cost for their Budget/Mid-range/Splurge group marked `price-level`. Drink listings are `evening` places. `parseTips` keeps the first two sentences of Stay safe and Get around. Guide picks rank first in the free real-places plan and in AI grounding (`mergePlaces` drops the same place from Wikipedia/OpenStreetMap by name); they are credited with a "Read the full travel guide" link.
- Reviews (`src/lib/reviews.ts`): Google reviews, Reddit and Tripadvisor are links to each site's own search, shown for real places in the itinerary and for the destination on the Overview. Their review text is never scraped, stored or sent to the AI (their terms don't allow it without an agreement). Google review text through the Places API is possible for signed-in travelers but is a paid option that isn't on.
- Real-places plans are budget-aware: `buildRealItinerary({ mealBudgetPerPersonDay })` skips restaurants that would blow the food budget and puts bars and music venues in the evening, never at a meal.
- AI runs on Google's Gemini API (free tier) with the project's own key. The key is the environment variable `GEMINI_API_KEY` (Vercel) (`geminiKey()` also accepts `GOOGLE_API_KEY` and similar spellings and strips pasted quotes; create one free at aistudio.google.com/apikey). Calls go through `src/lib/ai/gateway.server.ts` to Gemini's OpenAI-compatible endpoint (`@ai-sdk/openai` chat, structured output via JSON schema, `reasoning_effort` mapped to thinking): `generateText` with `maxRetries: 0` (not streaming, so errors keep their status and message) and `withFallbacks()`, which tries the task's model and then each free fallback once (`AI_FALLBACK_MODELS`; default `gemini-3.5-flash`, `gemini-3.8-flash`, `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`), because free quotas are per model and the newest models are often "high demand" (503). Busy (503) and quota (429) move on to the next model; a wrong key (Gemini says 400 "API key not valid", read as 401), 403 and 402 stop at once; a cancel or timeout is never retried. Failures return the friendly message plus `detail` (the service's own reason, keys stripped by `aiErrorInfo`), shown small under the error so problems can be reported.
- AI pipeline: models and reasoning effort come from `src/lib/ai/models.server.ts` (`AI_PLANNER_MODEL`, `AI_CHAT_MODEL`, `AI_PLANNER_EFFORT`, `AI_CHAT_EFFORT` secrets; default `gemini-3.6-flash` at low effort; only Gemini or Gemma ids are accepted, "google/" or "models/" prefixes are dropped, and old gateway ids such as "openai/gpt-6-astra" are ignored). After the first answer the server runs `planProblems()` (missing/thin days, a sight repeated on different days, sightseeing or meals well over budget) and, only if it finds problems and the first answer took under 40s, asks once for a corrected plan and keeps whichever has fewer problems. A price published by the source (`costSource: "listed"`) always replaces the model's guess in `cleanAiPlan`. Testing against the real API from a cloud session: the key is a network secret for generativelanguage.googleapis.com, so run with `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 GEMINI_API_KEY=proxy-injected`.
- Typed cities are looked up once typing pauses (`useDebouncedValue`, 300ms), so suggestions and the destination don't jump mid-word.
- Destination photos (`useDestinationSummary`): `photoQueries()` tries an area by its own name before its main town, and `parseSummary(body, near)` picks the search result really about the place: never a film, album or other namesake, and when the destination's location is known, an article whose coordinates are within 350 km. Better no photo than one of somewhere else.
- Only the costs a traveler actually has: `TripSetup.coverage` (`TripCoverage` in `budget.ts`: `lodging`, `meals`, `activities`, `localTransport`, `transfer`) marks what's already taken care of (staying at home or with family, eating at home, just getting there, a ride at the destination) and the round trip from their door (e.g. a dorm) to the airport or station. `applyCoverage()` zeroes the covered inputs and adds `transferRoundTrip` to Transportation; it's applied everywhere inputs are built (`buildTrip`, `suggestedBudget`, `setTransportMode`, the Plan preview), so covered costs never come back. The Plan page has "What you pay for" (`CoveragePicker`); the trip assistant fills `covered` and `transferCost` from what the traveler says ("I live there, just the flight and a ride from my dorm") and says what it left out; `coverageFromDraft()` maps it (an airport trip only for flights, trains and buses). Example plans follow it (`fitToCoverage`: meals at home, no paid sights; the traveler's own stops untouched), the AI planner is told what's covered, the Budget page explains each $0 line, and booking skips hotels, dinner reservations and tickets that aren't needed and adds "Ride to the airport".
- Totals are never a mystery: `transportParts()` in `budget.ts` splits Transportation into named lines (Flights · $300 × 2 round trips, Rental car, Getting around there, Ride to the airport), shown under Transportation on the Overview and in the Plan preview (only while Transportation isn't a custom amount). The misc category is labeled "Price buffer" and says what it's for. Once flight prices are connected, the Flights line links to "Check the real fare" (`/budget#live-prices`) and says "Live" after.
- Live prices need free keys set as Vercel environment variables: `SERPAPI_API_KEY` (Google Flights and Google Hotels through SerpApi; its free plan has a monthly search allowance, so searches stay on a button press) and `EIA_API_KEY` (U.S. gas prices, free). Live hotel prices are never searched or applied when the traveler has a place to stay (`coverage.lodging`).
- Price keys are read by `priceKey()` in `src/lib/prices/keys.server.ts`, which also accepts common spellings (`SERPAPI_KEY`, `SERP_API_KEY`, `EIA_KEY`...) and strips pasted spaces or quotes. The Budget page's live-prices panel explains setup when no key is found, and signed-in travelers can "Test connection" (`testPriceKeys`, auth-protected): SerpApi's account endpoint (uses no searches; shows searches left) and EIA's metadata route. It reports which secret name a key was found under, never the key.
- Setup instructions (variable names, Vercel steps) appear only in local development (`import.meta.env.DEV`); visitors to the published site see a plain line instead. Page notes go under the title, never floating beside it; the Budget page folds categories at $0 behind "Show N more at $0"; road-trip stop cards show whole dollars ("Under $1" for a few cents of detour gas) and only warn when a stop would go over budget.
- Groups go up to `MAX_TRAVELERS` (20, in `budget.ts`) everywhere: the Plan stepper, `setupFromDraft` and the assistant.
- Live price searches are cached on the server (`src/lib/prices/cache.server.ts`: in-memory plus Cloudflare's `caches.default` when present, both best-effort) so an identical search within a few hours spends no SerpApi searches: flights and hotel listings 3h (hotel listings are shared by every travel style; the style's pick is made after), gas 12h, airport codes 7 days. Only answers are cached, never failures; keys hold places and dates, never API keys. `fetchedAt` is the oldest part's real search time and `reused` says the toast should show how old the prices are (`reusedNote`).
- Sharing (`src/lib/share.ts`, `ShareTripButton`, `/shared`): the trip is packed into the link's `#trip=` fragment (deflate-raw + base64url, "j" plain fallback), so it needs no account or database and never reaches the server. `sharedTripPayload()` leaves out bookings, the account link, the trip id, fetched price listings and `originPoint` (exact coordinates); the start city's name stays. Links are untrusted: `decodeTrip()` caps sizes and `cleanSharedTrip()` keeps only http(s) links and clamps the group size before `tripActions.openSharedTrip()` opens it as a new trip of the traveler's own. The share button only shows for a real trip (not the sample); the shared page is `noindex`.

- Speed: tabs never wait on the network. The router preloads a page's code on hover/touch (`defaultPreload: "intent"`) and `AppShell` warms every tab once the first page is idle. Page changes are instant (no crossfade: they happen dozens of times a day). Formatters are made once and reused (`formatMoney`, `formatCalendarDate`): building an `Intl` formatter per call was a measurable share of each page render. Long card lists (itinerary days, road-trip stops, booking items) use `render-lazy` (`content-visibility: auto`) so off-screen cards aren't laid out until scrolled to.
- Security baseline: every server function that costs money or spends the shared free AI quota (AI planner and assistant, live prices, price-key test) requires sign-in and is rate-limited per account (`src/lib/rate-limit.server.ts`, `takeRate(userId, name)`; sliding hourly windows in memory, a guard against runaway use since sign-up is instant). Over the limit they return their normal `{ ok: false, error }` shape (status 429 for AI) with a plain "try again in about N minutes". `src/server.ts` adds `x-content-type-options`, `referrer-policy` and `permissions-policy` to every response and always drops a client-sent `x-waypoint-visitor-geo`. Every link built from outside data goes through `safeExternalUrl()` (http/https only) at render. Database tables (`trips`, `traveler_preferences`, `bookings`; migrations in `drizzle/migrations/`) have row-level security with owner-only policies and no anonymous grants; `client.server.ts` (service role) is unused and must stay server-only.
- Budgets never start negative (`fitBudget()` in `src/lib/trip/budget-fit.ts`, run by `tripActions.startTrip`, which returns a note): with no budget from the traveler, Waypoint picks one that covers the estimated costs plus the emergency fund and marks it `setup.budgetAuto`; a budget the traveler gave is kept when it covers the trip and otherwise raised, with the note saying so. The budget is fitted after the Plan page has located the destination and every city (`locateAll`), so real distances can't push it under. While `budgetAuto`, `rebudget()` re-fits it when the trip's shape changes (trip type, travel mode, cabin) and only raises it when a cost goes up (live prices, custom amounts, stops); `setTotalBudget` makes it the traveler's own and Waypoint stops adjusting it. The Plan page's budget field follows the suggestion until the traveler types a number. The assistant only sets `totalBudget` when the traveler gives one.
- Multi-city trips (`src/lib/multi-city.ts`): the destination is the first city and `setup.cities` lists the rest in order with nights (`TripCity`; up to `MAX_CITIES` = 5). `citySchedule()` gives each city its nights and arrival day (the destination keeps the rest, at least one; cities that don't fit drop off the end) and `tripLegs(setup)` is the one way to get them (cruises and staycations are always one place). `destinationFor()` prices the route as one-way legs home → cities → home (`routeLegMiles`, `faresForLegs`, 55% of a round trip each; unknown places count 250 miles). Plans are made per city and joined by `stitchCityPlans()` (travel stop and "Travel to X" title on each new city's first day, unique ids): example plans (`generateTripItinerary`), free real places and Google places per city, and the AI planner gets `cities` ("Days 1-3: Rome") plus places marked with their city. Booking lists a stay per city (`hotel`, `hotel-2`...) and multi-city flights; live prices price each flight leg one way and each city's hotels (nightly averaged by nights). The trip bar, Overview title and Plan preview show the route (`place` from `useTrip`). The assistant fills `cities` (every city the traveler named, in order) and works out dates from them.
- Flight cabins (`Cabin`, `CABINS` in `demo-data.ts`: economy 1×, premium economy 1.6×, business 3.2×, first 5×; estimates) scale flight fares in `destinationFor`. Chosen on the Plan page and in Budget → Transportation (`tripActions.setCabin`), filled by the assistant, sent to live prices as Google Flights' `travel_class`, and named in booking searches.
- Airports (`src/lib/airports.ts`, data in `src/data/airports.json` generated by `python -m waypoint_data airports` from OurAirports, public domain: large and medium airports with scheduled service and an IATA code, ~3,200): `airportFor(list, point)` picks the airport a traveler would use (a large hub within 45 miles of a nearer regional field wins; nothing past 220 miles), `airportByName` covers places whose location isn't known yet ("Charlotte, NC" → CLT), `airportByCode` checks typed codes. The table is loaded on demand in the browser (`useAirports`, `useTripAirports(homeAirport)` for home + each city; the profile's home airport wins). Airport codes go into booking searches and titles, live prices (`originAirport`, `destinationAirport`, each city's `airport`, so the SerpApi airport lookup is only a fallback), the Plan preview ("CLT → AVL", or a warning when a place has no airport nearby), the profile (nearest airports to pick from), the AI planner (where each city's flights land and how far that is) and the trip assistant (their nearest airport).
- AI grounding also includes: sunset times from the forecast in each day's weather line, the destination's region and country, how they travel (cabin, own car, transit), the local currency with today's rate, groups of 6+, and travel-guide tips from every city. Request lines are clipped to the server's limits (notes 160, weather 120, guide 360 characters) so a long line never gets a request refused.
- Trip length: any range up to a year (`MAX_TRIP_DAYS` = 365), planned any time ahead. Example and real-places plans cover every day. The AI planner takes up to 14 days per request (`SINGLE_REQUEST_DAYS`); longer trips are split by `planChunks()` (`src/lib/ai-chunks.ts`: parts of at most 10 days, evened out, never across a city change, a travel day flagged on each new city's first part), run three at a time, each with its own share of the city's places (`shareOf`) and a note saying which part it is, and joined with `joinChunkPlans()`; a failed part fails the whole plan (the current plan stays). Each part counts toward the hourly AI limit (60). Weather for dates more than a year out uses the latest year whose record exists (`recordYearsBack`); holidays cover every year the trip touches. Live flight and hotel prices aren't searched before they're listed (about 11 months ahead, `BOOKING_WINDOW_DAYS`); the panel says why. The itinerary shows the first 10 days at once and the rest in quick steps (all at once for printing).
- Code structure: pure logic lives in `src/lib` as small modules with tests; React only renders and calls actions. The trip store is split by job: `src/lib/trip/types.ts` (the stored shape), `build.ts` (new trips, cities, `planKey`, stops' cost), `budget-fit.ts` (`fitBudget`, `rebudget`, `suggestedBudget`), `migrate.ts` (old saved shapes, date repair) and `view.ts` (`viewTrip`: everything pages show, derived once). `trip-store.ts` keeps only the state, persistence, cross-tab sync and `tripActions`, and re-exports the rest so pages import from one place; pure modules import from `src/lib/trip/*` directly, never from the store. Browser code never imports a `*.server.ts` module (ESLint enforces it; type imports are allowed): server work goes through `createServerFn` in `*.functions.ts`. Run `bun run check` (types, lint, tests, data tools) before pushing.
- Data and Python (`src/data/`, `tools/`): reference data is JSON, not code: `airports.json` (generated; each airport's town in English with its other names, e.g. Firenze for Florence, from `citynames.py`, plus the state in the US, Canada and Australia), `countries.json` and `states.json` (generated from OurAirports' country and region tables by `python -m waypoint_data regions`), `places.json`, `cruises.json` and `place-aliases.json` (edited by hand, one record per line) and `rules.json` (the vocabulary and limits the data must follow, including the everyday words that are also town names; `src/test/data-files.test.ts` keeps it equal to the app's own types, and `MAX_AIRPORT_MILES` reads it). The Python tools (`tools/waypoint_data`, standard library only, managed with uv; pytest and ruff for development) build and check it: `python -m waypoint_data check` (ids, coordinates, interests, trip types, tiers, country codes, no long dashes, text limits, unknown fields, and an airport within reach of every place suggested for flights), `fmt` (the canonical layout; Prettier skips these files), `airports airports.csv`, `regions countries.csv regions.csv` and `prompts [--check]` (the assistant's 10,000-case test set). CI runs them in its own job. Python is for build-time data work only: the app and its server functions run on Cloudflare Workers, which run JavaScript, so runtime code stays TypeScript. To add a place, add a line to `places.json` and run `bun run data:check`.
- Design psychology (honest only: no fake scarcity, countdowns, invented reviews or social proof). The Plan page leads with one question ("Where to next?"), what Waypoint does (priced to the dollar) and one thing to do: the assistant in its `variant="hero"` with quick starts phrased as actions ("Plan a mountain weekend for 2"), so the first screen has one thing to do (Hick's law); the phone's pinned "Plan trip" bar only appears once the manual steps are in view. "Ideas from [home]" (`plan/Inspiration.tsx`) shows photo cards with how far each place is and the real estimate for the traveler's own dates and group (`estimatedSpend`), a drive and a flight alternating. A trip in progress gets "Pick up where you left off" at the top of the Plan page. The Overview leads with the destination photo, large, magazine style (`DestinationHero`, nothing when there's no photo), then the money; right after a trip is built (`markJustPlanned` in `src/lib/just-planned.ts`) it opens with "Your trip is planned" (`TripReady`: the route draws in, what was made, two next steps), shown once per trip until dismissed. The checklist shows a progress bar and names the next step (goal gradient).
- Main airports: `tools/waypoint_data/citynames.py` lists each city's main international hub (`PRIMARY_HUBS`: FCO, LHR, CDG, JFK, ORD...), written as size "H" in `airports.json` (`Airport.hub`); `airportFor()` gives hubs a 30-mile edge over the city's other large airports, so Rome flies from Fiumicino, not Ciampino.
- No hotel means no hotel money: the assistant's `prefs.ts` reads every way of saying it ("we don't need a hotel", "no hotel needed", "staying with a friend", "the hotel is paid by work", "we already booked an Airbnb", "dont need accommodation") as `covered.lodging`, so lodging is $0 everywhere (budget, booking list, live hotel searches); a question ("is the hotel booked?") or wanting one ("we need a hotel near the beach") never counts. The AI is told the same, and `reconcile()` keeps the reading.
- Flight stops (`FlightStops` in `demo-data.ts`: any, one stop at most, nonstop; `setup.flightStops`, `tripActions.setFlightStops`, `StopsPicker` next to the cabin on the Plan and Budget pages): the assistant reads "nonstop only", "1 layover is fine", "layovers are fine" and asks once on flight trips (before cabin); live searches pass Google Flights' `stops` filter (in the cache key), booking links carry it (Google query, KAYAK `fs=stops=0`, Skyscanner `preferDirects`) and the flight card says "Nonstop only" or "1 stop max". Fare estimates don't change with it.
- Passports, entry rules and layovers: `src/data/visas.json` (built by `python -m waypoint_data visas` from the Passport Index dataset, MIT, with its last-updated date; checked in CI) says what each passport needs to enter each country (`entryRule`, `entryText` in `src/lib/visas.ts`, loaded on demand with `useVisas`). The traveler's passport is `setup.passport` (home country when unset; never put in share links), chosen in the Book page's Layovers section. "Before you go" states the real entry rule for their passport. `layoverAdvice()` (`src/lib/layovers.ts`, tested) says whether they can leave the airport on their passport, how long they'd have outside (minus 90 minutes at home, 3 hours abroad for immigration and security), what to do inside (lounges, showers, transit hotels) and special cases (everyone connecting in the US or Canada clears immigration; China's visa-free transit; Istanbul's Touristanbul for those who can enter; airport transit visas), and never promises time outside to someone who can't leave. Live flight results keep each connection (`FlightOption.layovers`) and show "via IST 9h 20m"; the Layovers section advises on those and on any airport the traveler types. Every answer links to IATA Travel Centre to confirm.
- Spending (`/spending`, the "Spent" side of Budget via `BudgetTabs`; the Budget tab stays current there): what was actually spent lives in `trip.spending` (`people`, `expenses`), pure logic in `src/lib/spending.ts` with tests in `src/test/spending.test.ts`. `spendingSummary()` compares spent with planned per category (taxes folded into the categories they're charged on, so the categories add up to the plan to the cent; costs under a category the trip no longer uses count as Other), counts booking prices marked booked as spent (no typing twice), and during the trip shares what's left over the days to come, today included. The emergency fund is shown separately and spending never counts against it. Costs can be typed in the destination's or display currency (only offered once a rate is in; stored in US dollars with the original amount kept); in a group, `balances()` / `settleUp()` split each cost equally to the cent among who shared it and list the fewest payments to even up. Costs are edited, removed with Undo and downloaded as a spreadsheet (`spendingCsv`, formula cells kept as text). Spending is cleaned on load (`cleanSpending`), never put in share links, and the Overview shows `SpendingGlance` once the trip is under way or costs are logged.
- Place accuracy: a name's state or country decides which place it is. `matchDestination()` only matches a catalog place whose state or country fits what was typed ("Athens, GA" is never Athens, Greece; "Portland, ME" never Portland, Oregon), "uk" and non-word codes ("bc", "qc", "wa", "pa") qualify a town without a comma, a code two countries share means the US state, "Georgia" from a US traveler is the state ("tbilisi georgia" and "the country of georgia" are the country), and the same name in two countries stays two places. Golden cases cover each one.
- Best places first: `fetchLandmarks()` runs two Wikipedia searches (the most-linked articles near the place, sorted by incoming links, and the nearest ones) with 30 days of page views, and `parseLandmarks()` ranks by `fameScore()` (views, else article length, a little less for distance); `classifySight()` knows what travelers go to see (amphitheatres, fountains, statues, steps, ruins, historic buildings) and what they don't (stations, embassies, offices). Views are kept as `RealPlace.fame`, carried through `mergePlaces`, used by `rankSights()`, by `placeScore()` in `buildRealItinerary` and told to the AI planner ("one of the best-known sights here"). Restaurants from OpenStreetMap are ranked by `restaurantScore()` (a Wikipedia entry, website, hours, cuisine, near the center); chains and brands sink.
- Towns (`python -m waypoint_data towns cities500.txt /usr/share/dict/words`): GeoNames (CC BY 4.0) towns of 50,000+, 15,000+ in the countries travelers visit most, and a hand-picked list of small destinations (`MUST_HAVE`: Sedona, Hallstatt, Positano...); towns the airport table has, city neighborhoods (within 8 km of a place 10 times bigger) and names that are never towns (small words, months, seasons, trip words: `NEVER`) are left out; Japanese "-shi" is dropped. Each row says whether the name is an everyday word (from a word list where common words are lowercase). Loaded on demand (its own chunk, about 280 KB gzipped). `check` validates it and audits every catalog place and cruise port against where its town really is (`check_locations`, within 60 km, by state in the US).
